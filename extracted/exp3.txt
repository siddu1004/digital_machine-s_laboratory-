SPEED CONTROL OF 3-PHASE INDUCTION MOTOR 
 
AIM: 
1 To control the speed of the given 3 phase squirrel cage induction motor by pole changing method. 
2 To control the speed of the given slip ring induction motor by changing rotor resistance and to plot 
the variations of speed for different values of rotor resistance. 
3 To control the speed of the given 3_phase slip ring induction motor by varying the input voltage 
and to plot the variation of speed with voltage at no load and 25% full load. 
 
INSTRUMENTS AND ACCESSORIES: 
Sl NO: ITEM TYPE AND RANGE QUANTITY 
1 Auto transformer   
2 Voltmeter   
3 Ammeter   
4 Tachometer   
 
THEORY: 
The synchronous speed of an induction motor is given by Ns=120f/P. So by changing the no. of poles on 
the stator, the synchronous speed and hence rotor speed can be changed. The no. of stator and rotor poles 
should be equal. The stator winding can be designed so that by a simple change in coil connections, the 
no. of poles can be changed. As the no. of poles on the stator is changed, a cage rotor adjusts itself to the 
same number of poles. The stator winding can be connected for 2-pole, 4-pole or 6-pole configuration as 
shown in the diagram. 
The speed of the slip ring induction motor can be changed by changing the rotor resistance. By changing 
rotor resistance the same load torque can be obtained at different speed. For a given load torque as rotor 
resistance increases the speed decreases and vice versa. Even though with simple method high starting 
torque can be obtained, this has the drawback of the power loss in the external resistance. 
As the torque developed by the induction motor is proportional to square of the voltage, the speed can 
also be varied by changing the voltage.

PROCEDURE: 
1. POLE CHANGING METHOD 
I. Make connection as shown in the circuit diagram. 
II. Using a 3-phase auto transformer, slowly increase the voltage to the rated value. 
III.  Measure and record the voltage applied and the speed on no load.  
IV.  Switch off the power supply. 
V. Repeat the same procedure for 4-pole and 2-pole configurations. 
 
2. ROTOR RHEOSTATIC SPEED  CONTROL 
I. Make a connection as shown in the circuit diagram 
II.  Keep the rotor rheostat at a minimum resistance position and give supply to the stator. 
The motor should be under no load. 
III. Motor starts rotating, and the ammeter, voltmeter, and tachometer readings are noted 
down. 
IV. Rotor resistance is gradually increased  and at each step, all the meter readings are 
observed and tabulated. 
V. Experiment is repeated until the maximum value of rotor circuit resistance is reached. 
 
3.  SPEED CONTROL BY CHANGING THE VOLTAGE 
I. Connections are made as shown in circuit diagram 
II. Switch on the supply with the motor under no load and keep the autotransformer in the 
minimum voltage position. 
III. Apply 20% normal voltage and note down the no load speed. 
IV. No load speed is noted with 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% normal voltage. 
V. Experiment is repeated with 25%load on the motor, and the above procedure is repeated. 
VI. Reduce the load and voltage and switch off the motor supply.
