NO LOAD AND BLOCKED ROTOR TESTS ON 3-PHASE SQUIRREL CAGE INDUCTION 
MOTOR 
Aim : 
1. To conduct the no-load test and blocked rotor test on the given 3-phase cage induction motor and to 
obtain the values of equivalent circuit parameters. 
2. Using the equivalent circuit to calculate the following at any given slip 
 a) Current drawn from the supply 
 b) Input power factor 
 c) Torque developed 
d) Approximate output 
e) Efficiency 
3. To calculate the slip for maximum power and maximum power. 
4. Draw the circle diagram of the three-phase induction motor from test data.

PROCEDURE: 
1. NO-LOAD TEST 
(1)  Make the connections as shown in the diagram. 
(2) With no load on the motor and with the autotransformer set for zero output voltage, switch on the 
supply. 
(3) Slowly increase the voltage to the normal rated value. 
(4)  While increasing the voltage, see that the current drawn by the motor never exceeds the full load 
value. 
(5) Note the no-load voltage, current, speed, and input power. 
(6) Reduce the voltage to zero and switch off the supply. Note that the no-load current drawn by the motor 
is 20-30% of the full load value, and the no-load power factor will usually be less than 0.2. Hence an LPF 
wattmeter is used for power measurement. 
 
2. BLOCK ROTOR TEST: 
(1) Connections are made as shown in the diagram. Usually, about 30% rated voltage is sufficient for 
circulating full-load current through the stator winding. The power factor under blocked–rotor condition 
is well above 0.5, and hence a UPF wattmeter is to be used. 
(2) Prevent the rotor from rotation by mechanical loading. 
(3)  With the autotransformer set for zero output voltage, switch on the supply. 
(4) Increase the voltage to a value sufficient to circulate the full-load current through stator windings. 
(5) Note down the input power, current, and voltage applied. 
(6) Reduce the voltage and switch off the supply and release the load on the motor. 
 
3. STATOR RESISTANCE MEASUREMENT 
(1) Make the connections to measure stator resistance. 
(2) The DC resistance per phase of the stator winding is determined by the usual voltmeter-ammeter 
method.
